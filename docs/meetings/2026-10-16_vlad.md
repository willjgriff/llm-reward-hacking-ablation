**Cosine similarity**
- X2 is close to orthogonal to the original TerminalVerifier direction.
- For two random Gaussian vectors in Qwen3.8-27B's 5,120-dimensional residual stream, the typical cosine is about 0.014. That is 1/√d, the RMS; the expected |cos| is 0.011. Vlad noted our cosines are 5–7× that.
- High-dimensional intuition misleads here: low cosine is the norm. Compare against unrelated concept directions (speak Spanish vs French, refusal).

**p-values**
- The p-value is the probability of seeing an effect at least this large if there were no real difference.
- Below 0.001: trust it. Below 0.05: probably real, but rerun if it's load-bearing or was selected from many. 0.05–0.2: probably chance; rerun at about 5× the sample if cheap and high priority. Above 0.2: chance.
- Multiple comparisons: with 20 experiments, about one reaches p < 0.05 by chance. Picking the significant ones out of many is p-hacking.
- Have Claude compute p-values carefully, without false assumptions, and estimate the sample size needed.

**Within-environment orthogonalisation is unreliable**
- Training and testing on the same environment works well for TerminalVerifier, OK for MBPP, and increases hacking for the Grader. Vlad: "super cursed", inconclusive, warrants investigation.
- I said TerminalVerifier probably works because I spent far more time tuning the method there. That fits Vlad's prior.
- Steering works on the Grader but orthogonalisation does not. Vlad's prior: orthogonalisation is under-tuned, not fundamentally broken.
- If it can't be tuned to work even within one environment, better to find out early and pivot.

**Data diversity**
- If self-transfer works much better than cross-environment transfer, and cosines are small compared with baselines, the data is probably not diverse enough.
- Vlad asked whether I had tried multiple configurations of one environment: transfer from each to the others, and from all-but-one to the held-out one. I said I could try it.
- The hack types differ conceptually:
  - TerminalVerifier `verifier_bug`: the "hack" is fixing a verifier bug. It is very unclear whether that should count as a hack.
  - `corrupted` TerminalVerifier and impossible MBPP: the tasks are impossible, which is arguably different from hacking on solvable tasks.
  - Grader: not a coding environment, and the model is artificially shown hints about the grader.
- The model may represent each hack type as its own direction, with no clean shared one. Vlad had no specific action from this; it's something to keep in mind.
